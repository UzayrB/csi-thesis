**Why that is the causal reading of rule 2a.** At the delayed press moment (press + 2.0 s in video

starts at the wrong line's entry").

line". Nothing was changed.

## 5d. A proposed "decoded accuracy" for the videos, consistent with V1 (RENDER_RULES not edited)

**What the files report now** (f3's and f4's inherited definition, `pd_render.py` line 236). The dot at
video time x is compared with the truth at x - 1.0 s. Under V1 the screen shows the moment x - 2.0 s, so
this compares the dot with where the walker was 1 s AFTER the moment on screen. It is 1.9 to 2.3 percent
within 0.5 m, and it measures nothing the display promises.

**The proposal.**
- **Decoded accuracy (rule 4).** On each frame from the walk's first decoded position to its end, the
  distance between the red dot and the ground truth at x - 2.0 s: the scene moment V1 says the frame
  shows, and the moment the green marker shows.
  - **Exclusions:** the parked frames and the rule 2a anchor segment, as rule 2b already requires.
  - **Reported as:** the median and 90th percentile in metres, and the share of frames within 0.5 m.
    These are given per walk and pooled, and separately for walks whose lock is right and wrong.
  - **What it already equals:** `pd_render`'s "on-screen tracking against the delayed marker",
    43.5 percent (A3) and 48.9 percent (B2) within 0.5 m.
- **Display offset (rule 4, reported apart and never added).** On the same frames, the distance between
  the truth at x - 2.0 s and the truth at x: how far the walker moves during the fixed delay. At a
  1.3 m/s pace this is about 2.6 m.
- **The inherited x - 1.0 s figure** is retired.
- **The decoder's own resolution** (`pd_decode.drawn_errors`: the dot against the truth at its window's
  label time) stays the headline resolution figure. It measures the decoder, not the video.

**Why this fits V1 and V3.** The latency is one fixed constant. So the scene moment of every frame is
fixed by construction, and needs nothing measured per walk.
