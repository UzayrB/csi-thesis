# THE MULTI-HEAD POOL AND THE REPAIRED LIVE RENDER, 2026-09-19

The batch in which the pre-registered bar was passed for the first time, and in which
the demonstration was found to have been showing the wrong thing and was repaired.

## Part one: the pool


This is the batch in which the pre-registered bar was passed for the first time. The live mode goes
from 46.7 to 57.5 percent cross-day and the gap to bounded closes from 11.7 points to 8.3, against
a ten-point bar that was never reworded. Frozen at `runs/TRACKING_FROZEN_2026-09-18m.json`;
results at `runs/constrained/fh/g3_final/results.json`.

## What was changed, which is less than it sounds

Nothing in the decoder. No retraining. The only change is what the decoder READS: instead of one
encoder's posterior over cells, it reads the equal-weight log-opinion pool of five. The end rule,
the entry rule, the rescore, the twelve chains and the audits are byte-for-byte the arm that
shipped on 18 September.

## Why this worked when seven other levers had not

Between 17 and 19 September the project tried an arc-fraction lockout, a maximum-speed cap, a
time-based lockout, an exit parameter sweep, a candidate-end rescore, prior flattening, pinned
entry and day-only calibration. Every one of them attacked the END, because the diagnosis said the
end is where the whole gap lives: half the passes end more than a second from the operator's press
and those score 21.1 percent against the good half's 73.4. Each lever that bought points bought
them by making the end partly a function of elapsed time, and the substitution test refused each
one by name.

Pooling heads is the first lever that does not touch the end at all. Independently trained
encoders make errors that are partly particular to their own training run; averaging their log
posteriors cancels that part and keeps the part they agree on. The decoder then does exactly what
it did before, on a cleaner emission. **There is no mechanism by which a sharper emission can
smuggle in the clock**, which is why every pooled arm passes the guard while several end-directed
arms did not. The standing sentence is therefore: nothing which attacks the end directly reaches
the bar without turning the end into a clock, and the thing that does reach the bar never attacks
the end at all.

## The rule the pool was built under, fixed before the numbers

**Every available head, equal weight.** Using every head equally is parameter-free. Choosing the
best three by looking at results is a selection, and selection was measured on this very data to
hurt: a weighted arm that tuned the mix scored 50.8 against the equal-weight 60.0. No head is
dropped for scoring badly, and the g100 region head is in the pool despite being the one that costs
points on one reading.

## Latency, against the 500 ms contract

One encoder pass over one link costs **3.16 ms** on this machine, single threaded, batch of one,
which is the pessimistic case; a deployment would batch the three links. Five heads over three
links is fifteen passes, **47.4 ms**, plus **2.41 ms** of decoding, for **49.8 ms per window**
against a **500 ms** contract. The per-link and decode times are measured; the shipped-head total
is those multiplied out. **Latency is not a constraint on how many heads the pool carries**, which
matters because it means the curve saturated for statistical reasons and not for engineering ones.

## What this does not fix

The end. Pooling raised the line without improving the end, so the diagnosis of 19 September still
stands in full: half the passes have an interval more than a second from the press, and the
stops-short tail is 85 percent of the failures. More heads is now closed as a direction, because
the curve saturates at three and latency was never the limit. If more heads are wanted they must
differ in KIND, as the g100 region head does, rather than in seed.

## What the freeze caught on its first attempt, and why 18j is kept

The first freeze, **18j, FAILED its own verification** and records that rather than hiding it. Two
standing tests fired. Neither touched a reported number and both were real.

**The pass-pairing audit flagged `g2_multihead.py`** for keying a dict on `pass_id` alone. On
inspection it is safe: the dict is built from one recording's bounded decode and consumed inside
that same iteration, and the rows it emits carry `stem|pass_id`. It is now classified with that
reason, so the bounded figures of 65.8 and 66.7 are correctly paired. The test was right to refuse
it until somebody said which kind it was, which is exactly what it was written to do.

**The render-truncation audit flagged twelve missing videos.** They were the bounded set that the
live render superseded, moved to `testing/demo_retired/2026-09-19/` with a manifest, never deleted.
The test could not tell a deliberately retired video from one the renderer claimed and never wrote.
It now distinguishes them by looking for a retirement manifest, so a genuinely missing video still
fails.

**The same fix closed a real hole.** The truncation test only ever read the bounded render's
results, so it was checking ZERO of the six live videos; it now checks all six, at about 445 bytes
per frame. A test that passed while proving nothing about the current render is worse than one that
fails, and the strengthened version asserts that it checked something.

18j is kept unaltered beside 18k, in the same way 18d was kept on 18 September: dated evidence that
the framework caught something before it shipped.

## Part two: the render, repaired


The demonstration that was frozen at 18k had no red dot in it. Diagnosing that turned up three
defects in the renderer, all of which had survived a green suite, a contract check, a verification
stage and a freeze. None of them changed a reported accuracy figure; all three changed what a
viewer would have believed about the system.

## 1. The dot was dead code, not a timing problem

The dot's position was computed into a local variable and never assigned to the artist.
`rdot.set_data` appeared exactly once in the whole renderer, in the branch that CLEARS it between
walks. The trail had the same shape of defect: its point lists were cleared and never appended to.
The renderer was not waiting for a commit before drawing, which was the plausible guess; it never
drew at all.

## 2. The causality assertion had never asserted anything

The stage initialised a violation counter to zero, computed a bound, counted frames, and never
compared the two. "0 violations over 7490 frames" was a frame count. The module docstring calls
that assertion the point of the stage.

It is now a real check, and deliberately not a restatement of the arithmetic that produced the
index: for every frame the CENTRE TIME of the window being read is compared against that frame's
own timestamp minus the display lag, using the recording's real timestamps. 0 violations over
**4063 frames actually checked**. The far edge of a read window never reaches past the frame being
drawn at all, an overhang of 0.0 s, which is reported rather than asserted because the shipped
contract is written on the index.

## 3. The highlighted line was drawn from the truth

The worst of the three. The shown line was `true if commit_s[d] else ""`: the TRUE label, displayed
whenever the decoder happened to be correct at that horizon, and blank otherwise. The video could
therefore never show a wrong line, only the right one or nothing. Another participant's CSI would
barely have changed what was drawn, which is this project's own standing test for whether a
component is signal-driven, applied in the one place nobody had applied it.

The decoder had the right object all along: `running_argmax_display` builds a post-hysteresis line
name per window and `night_track` was discarding it. It now also returns the best state WITHIN the
displayed chain per window, which is what the dot is drawn at, so the dot is a decoded position.

## What the repaired demonstration shows

Everything drawn during a walk is the semi-bounded decoder on the shipped five-head pool, from the
first window after the start press, at **display hysteresis one** so the line and the dot follow
the leading chain as soon as it leads. Each change is a 0.3 s slide with the switch counter
incrementing. At the operator's end press the track locks to the **bounded rescore on the same
pool**, labelled as matching or differing from the line shown just before.

| | |
|---|---|
| videos | 6, one per participant, one render run |
| bytes per frame | 540 to 625, all plausible |
| walks | 120, of which **118 contain at least one switch** |

| walks flagged for more than four switches | **0**, and flagging rather than smoothing is the rule |
| causality violations | **0 over 4063 frames actually checked** |
| committed on this pairing, bounded rescore on the pool | 78.3 percent |

**The display lag stays at 1 s** because the lag sweep showed a shorter lag costs position

## The standing test this earned

`tests/test_red_dot_is_drawn.py` fails any rendered walk whose red dot is absent for longer than
the display lag, fails a run that reports a zero gap on every walk (which would mean the counter is
not wired up), fails a causality count taken over zero frames, and bans by name the pattern that
drew the highlighted line from the true label. It was written against the old results first and
FAILED there, which is how it was checked to have teeth.

**The lesson, which is the same one this project keeps paying for.** Exit codes, contracts, byte
counts and a green suite all passed on a demonstration that showed the wrong thing. Every check in
place measured whether the stage ran, and none measured whether what it produced was what it
claimed. 18k is kept, not deleted, as the dated record of that.

# The rebuilt decoder, 2026-09-18

One model: a person walks one of twelve taped straight lines, or is not on a line at all.
Everything here is measured under the CORRECTED measurement of stage 1; nothing computed the
old way is quoted anywhere, and the figures the old way produced are withdrawn below.

## Framing and scope

**The headline result is the BOUNDARIES-GIVEN tracker, and it works CROSS-DAY.** Told when each walk starts and ends, and nothing else, it identifies which of the twelve lines was walked on 63.2 percent of passes over nine cross-day pairings, and 80.0 percent on the demonstration pairing at 0.305 m median error when correct. Two caveats travel with that number: the demonstration pairing trains on TWO sessions and tests on the third, making it the STRONGEST cross-day pairing rather than a typical one, so 63.2 percent is the quotable figure; and per participant it ranges from 60 to 95 percent, so a pooled number hides real variance.

Beneath it sits the SELF-SEGMENTING tracker, which must also detect that a walk began. That is a within-day system, and cross-day live is reported as an honest negative against a baseline defined in advance. The difference between 80 percent and 3 percent is the whole cost of having to find the walk.

The project's stated core contribution was cross-ENVIRONMENT generalisation through domain-adversarial training. That contribution is not delivered and the change of scope is stated here rather than left implicit. A second room was never recorded, so cross-environment generalisation is UNTESTED with the data that exists. It is NOT unreachable: A SECOND COLLECTION RUNS ON 26 AND 27 SEPTEMBER IN A DIFFERENT ROOM, and the protocol levers at the end of this report are written for it. Cross-DAY generalisation was tested thoroughly and is the negative result below: it resisted DANN four times, CORAL, contrastive learning, pseudo-labelling, mixup, phase input, drift-robust Doppler, the line-of-sight crossing code, and every decoder change in this batch.

### The within-day result is not contaminated. Checked before anything else was written.

The defect that withdrew the 91.1 percent identification figure was stage3_paths decoding BOTH sides of the within-day split, so 96 of its 120 scored passes were passes the model had been fitted on. The same question was put to the frozen decoder's within-day figures and they are CLEAN:

- `build_full_posteriors` trains on the within-day split's TRAINING side, the 96 passes. The held-out 24 are excluded from fitting.
- `rb1_truth` passes `held_out_passes(...)` to both the scorer and the identifier, so scoring is restricted to the 24.
- The two code paths agree: the row mask is built before and independently of whether features are loaded, verified equal on all three sessions with a train-held intersection of zero.
- Decisive: every within-day pairing reports **n_passes = 24, not 120**. stage3_paths reported 120. The defect is stage 3's alone.

The within-day figures rest on 72 held-out passes in total, 3 pairings. The intervals are correspondingly wide and are given with every figure; no within-day number should be read as precise.

**3 of the four edge lines were found zero times within-day (E_col1, E_row1, E_row5).** 

This is the MIRROR of cross-day, where the scaled emission puts the edge family first and collapses the diagonals. Both regimes are one-family results; they are simply different families. But the FAMILY is not the variable that matters, and calling this an edge-line problem was wrong: the gap between a line LEADING the hypothesis and being COMMITTED to tracks the number of PARALLEL SIBLINGS. The six lines with two siblings each lose about 26 points; the four diagonals, which have none, lose 6. Edge and central lines fail together because both have siblings. The completion batch section carries the measurement.

The held-out fifth is itself line-imbalanced by construction: a random one-in-five hold-out over 1.67 passes per line per participant leaves some lines with two or three passes in the whole within-day evaluation. That is evidence for the first protocol lever below.

## The four requirements, answered plainly

### V1. Measure honestly

WITHDRAWN under the corrected measurement: the old 'passes found' of 57 percent cross-day and 99 percent within-day at lag 3, and the demonstration pairing's 97.5 percent with 1.400 m and 25.8 percent path accuracy, all of which credited any on-path window within eight windows of a pass. Under the strict definition the same frozen decoder at lag 3 finds 11.9 percent cross-day and 45.8 percent within-day, with an all-windows positional error of 1.602 m cross-day.

The old metric counted a pass as found if the decoder was on ANY line anywhere within eight windows of it. Eight of the twelve lines cross the room centre and the decoder was on a line about thirty percent of the time, so a dot hovering at the centre scored every pass. A pass is now found only if the decoder occupied the CORRECT chain, reached its exit zone within two seconds of the true end, and covered at least half the pass's labelled windows. On the truth: the brief's centre-time labelling rule turned out to BE the existing rule, verified bit-identical on 17608 windows, so no label was extended and no head retrained. The 23 percent of each line that goes unscored is the one second label lattice, not a withheld label, and recovering it needs the finer stride of the optional 3.2b arm.

### V2. As little lag as the data allows

**Shipping display lag 1 second.** best strict pass-found is 8.0 percent at lag 2; its bootstrap band reaches down to 3.7; the shortest lag inside that band is 1. What each extra second bought: lag 0 to 1: strict found 1.1 to 5.3 percent, all-windows 1.812 to 1.801 m; lag 1 to 2: strict found 5.3 to 8.0 percent, all-windows 1.801 to 1.839 m.

Entry latency, which is what the viewer actually experiences and is NOT the lag, is a median 0.11 s and a p90 of 0.64 s cross-day. The display revises 8.75 times per pass at a mean catch-up of 2.30 m. Late entry is what makes a short lag viable at all: the frozen decoder finds 0.2 percent of passes at lag 1 because a chain could only be entered at its first state.

### V3. No firing when nobody is walking

The motion gate's stationary reading is a safe veto, because its walking recall is 100 percent. Stationary false occupancies per recording fall from 1.22 to 0.00 and the dwell block is held in null 97.0 percent of the time. TRANSIT fires are reported separately and honestly, at 24.7 per recording against the frozen decoder's 4.7: the operator has accepted transit as possibly irreducible, so it is a cost on record and never pooled into one precision figure.

### V4. The demonstration looks like real life

**The demonstration set is the BOUNDARIES-GIVEN render**, six recordings cross-day with 96 of 120 lines identified (80.0 percent) and 6 selected 60 to 90 s highlights beside the full archival renders. A visible WALK DECLARED cue fires at the moment the start is supplied, so a viewer can separate what the system was told from what it worked out. The dwell block is cut with a captioned 30 s excerpt kept, and during it the render states that the person is standing, that the system correctly detects nobody is walking, and that it does not estimate a standing position by design.

The self-segmenting render is kept only as a short CONTRAST clip, uncalibrated beside calibrated, and is captioned as such: it finds 4 of 120 passes cross-day, which matches its measured 3.33 percent floor exactly, so the render is correct and the mode is simply not demonstrable.

A render defect was diagnosed rather than adjusted: the red dot led the truth by a CONSTANT 1.270 m over 96 passes, trend -0.018 m/s. That ruled out a speed mismatch, since the implied 1.298 m/s is within 0.3 percent of the measured 1.302, and the magnitude named the cause, 1.270 m being one window's advance. A window's label is the position at its CENTRE time and the render anchored each state at the window's START, drawing it a second early. Anchor corrected, drawing rate untouched, mean offset now -0.004 m.

## The line-of-sight crossing code: a null result, written up as a finding

The premise was that a walker crossing a transmitter-receiver sight line leaves a dip at the fraction along the line that geometry predicts, so the order and position of those dips is a code that identifies the line, physical rather than learned and therefore day invariant. Every predicted fraction was recomputed from `pipeline/constrained/paths.py` and agreed with the brief on all twelve lines, including that `B` and `E_row5` are degenerate. The premise then failed at measurement, over 312 predicted crossings:

| | detection at the predicted fraction | false dips where geometry predicts nothing |
|---|---|---|
| RSSI | 16.3% | 9.3% |
| CSI amplitude | 23.4% | 39.2% |
| gate | 60.0% | |

Three things, each worth more than the headline. First, CSI amplitude fired MORE often where geometry predicts nothing (39.2 percent) than where it predicts a crossing (23.4 percent), which makes it anti-informative rather than merely weak. Second, detection was NOT day invariant, 10.3 and 4.5 percent on session 01 against 22.4 and 42.3 on session 03, and day invariance was the entire reason to prefer a geometric code over a learned one. Third, the dip threshold was PERMISSIVE rather than strict, minus one standard deviation held for 0.15 s, so this is not a sensitivity failure: the dips exist, and they are not where the geometry says. It is a MISLOCATION failure.

The physical reading. In a 5 m room the reflected paths carry energy around a body, so the direct-path shadow is partly filled rather than cut; and the first Fresnel radius is about 0.4 m, comparable to a person's width, so what remains of the shadow is smeared across roughly 0.6 s of walking, which at 1.298 m per second is most of the tolerance the test allows. RSSI was at least the right way round, detecting more often at predicted crossings than away from them, while CSI amplitude was not. That is consistent with the earlier finding that RSSI carried the y-axis information the geometry leaves RX1 unable to see.

## The half-second stride, amendment D1

At a 1 s stride the median pass yields FOUR labelled windows covering 76.5 percent of its line, and strict pass-found demands the correct chain, the exit zone, AND half the pass's windows. Four windows is a harsh lattice for that, so part of the low pass-found could be the measurement grid rather than the model. At 0.5 s the median pass yields NINE windows covering 92.9 percent. |---|---|---|
| 0.0 s | 1.11 % | 0.37 % |
| 1.0 s | 5.28 % | 5.56 % |

### The verdict, and the confound that limits it

**The 1 s arm ships. The 0.5 s arm is not re-swept.** Pass-found rises at the 2 s cap, cross-day 7.96 to 11.48 percent on 8 of 9 pairings and within-day 18.06 to 45.83 on 2 of 3, but transit firing rises 2.4 to 2.9 times in every cell and the positional margin against the centre baseline narrows or goes negative. Under the O2 rule it wins pass-found, loses false occupancies badly, and loses the tie-break.

**THE CONFOUND, and it is why this run does NOT answer the question it was built to ask.** The commitment threshold was tuned at the 1 s stride and was not re-tuned at 0.5 s. Halving the stride halves the evidence carried by each window, so the same accumulated threshold is reached in half the wall-clock time and the decoder commits EARLIER. Earlier commitment on its own is sufficient to explain all three movements: more passes entered and therefore more found, more transit segments entered and therefore more false firing, and a looser positional fit. The pass-found gain is bought by that aggressiveness, not demonstrably by finer temporal resolution.

So this report does NOT say the 0.5 s stride fails to help. It says a 0.5 s stride run at a threshold tuned for 1 s commits earlier, and that the question of whether the stride itself helps REMAINS OPEN. Re-sweeping the commitment threshold at the finer stride is recorded as future work, NOT RUN, and the reason it was not run is budget rather than result.

**Two readings of these tables that must not be made.** The within-day 1.0 s row shows 18.06 percent on both arms: that is 13 of 72 passes on each side, an exact identity in the count rather than a rounding coincidence, though the per-pairing composition differs (3, 5 and 5 on the finer arm). Read correctly: CROSS-DAY neither arm beats its null and the finer stride moves it further away; WITHIN-DAY both arms beat the null on 2 of 3 pairings, within_s01 failing in both, and the finer stride NARROWS that margin, from +0.242 to -0.062 m at 1.0 s and from +0.220 to +0.028 m at 2.0 s.

Its reference quantile is recomputed per recording so it adapts, but the offset is a learned constant in nats and does not transfer: the decoder fired 144.7 times per recording in transit against the 1 s arm's 19.4, and on another pairing went completely silent. ## A finding about the selection procedure itself

This is reported as one finding rather than as scattered footnotes, because it is systemic. Of eight training-side selections in this project, five were made IN SAMPLE and two were made ON THE TEST SIDE. Only one was clean, and it turned out inert.

| selection | verdict |
|---|---|
| commitment margin, both sweeps | in-sample |
| null self-transition and exit_k | in-sample |
| adaptive null rule (q, offset, gate weight) | in-sample |
| bout prior | in-sample |
| emission exponent alpha | in-sample |
| chain-length normalisation | test-side, inert: chose False on all twelve pairings |
| **emission: posterior against scaled** | **test-side, live** |
| **shipping lag** | **test-side, live** |

The mechanism for the in-sample five is a single one: the helper that builds the training side for a selection reads recordings of the TRAINING sessions, while the heads were fitted on every recording of those sessions, so the two sets overlap completely. In-sample selection is a methodological limitation. Selection on the TEST side is leakage into a reported number and is worse, so the two live cases are given verdicts individually.

**The emission choice.** Posterior against scaled likelihood, selected by summarising over the test aggregates; everything from stage 2 onward used the winner. It STANDS WITH A NARROW CAVEAT. The two differ by 1.4 points within-day (posterior 62.5 against scaled 61.1), and the headline within-day figures are reported from the POSTERIOR arm, the option that LOST the selection, so the within-day result carries no optimistic bias from this choice. Cross-day is different: scaled 12.5 percent against posterior 9.4 percent is a 3.1-point advantage selected on the test side, and that advantage is never quoted here as an unbiased effect size.

**The shipping lag.** Selected on the test aggregates, and every live figure depends on it. It STANDS WITH A CAVEAT, and the bias runs in the conservative direction: the rule chose the SHORTEST lag inside the band of the best rather than the argmax, so lag 2 scored higher on test and lag 1 was shipped anyway. What does carry bias is the claim that lag 1 is statistically indistinguishable from lag 2, because the band itself was computed on test. The lag-1 numbers are reported as measured; the indistinguishability is reported as test-selected.

**Neither requires withdrawal. Both carry the caveat above wherever they are quoted.**

Neither was ever completed: they appear as queued rows in the method ledger and are absent from its final version, and the module was archived as never imported by the final model. Those figures do not exist and are not cited. The verified evidence is the retained ablation WALKING LABELS AGAINST GRID DWELLS: 1.53 m against 1.81 m, same architecture, consistent across four architectures.

**THAT ABLATION IS ABOUT THE EVALUATION TARGET, NOT THE TRAINING SOURCE**, and the ledger says so in its own words: it 'changes the evaluation set, not the model'. It means a grid dwell is a harder thing to LOCALISE than a walking window. It does NOT mean grid data is a worse thing to TRAIN ON, and the static verification in this report shows the opposite: a regressor trained ONLY on grid windows beats the constrained decoder on walking windows on ALL SIX cross-day rows. The two findings are compatible and an earlier draft conflated them. Cutting the dwell grid is recommended on TIME-BUDGET grounds, to buy passes, and NOT because stationary data is a poor training source.

## What is defensible and what is not

**Defensible.** That the corrected measurement is what the old numbers should have been measured against, and that the old pass-found figures are withdrawn rather than quietly restated. That the gate veto removes stationary firing outright. That late entry is what makes a one second lag viable. That every committed track is a connected walk, asserted on every pairing.

That the live cross-day null is reported at all: the tracker is compared against a dot fixed at the centre of the room, on the same windows, with the baseline defined in advance. Very little published ESP32 work reports such a comparison, and reporting it is a contribution independent of whether the tracker beats it.

**Not defensible.** Any claim about a person walking freely. Any figure from the demonstration renders as the tracker's accuracy, since they are one pairing. Any cross-day figure near 100 percent. Any pooled twelve-line figure quoted without the family breakdown, in either regime. The cross-day advantage of the scaled emission, and the claim that lag 1 is indistinguishable from lag 2, both of which were selected on the test side. And nothing here is a clean held-out result: session 03 was opened long ago.

**The scope, stated plainly.** The project set out to show cross-ENVIRONMENT generalisation through domain-adversarial training. With the data that exists that claim is UNTESTED, because a second room was never recorded. It is NOT unreachable: a second collection runs on 26 and 27 September in a different room. What this report delivers is a boundaries-given tracker that works cross-day, a within-day self-segmenting tracker, and a thoroughly tested cross-day negative for the self-segmenting mode.

# The mid-line stall: measured, diagnosed, and the fix that did not ship

Added 2026-09-18. The operator, watching the boundaries-given renders, reported that for some
participants the red dot stops partway along the line and then sits still while the green dot
carries on, so red appears to lag green exactly and never reaches the 5 m mark. P3's renders were
the worst and P2's were described as near perfect. The question was whether that is the drawing or
the model.

The measurement confirms P3 as the worst of the six. It does not quite confirm the other half of
the impression: P2 comes out mid-table at 0.91 m short and 35 percent of passes tracked to the end,
while P5 is the one that is actually near perfect at 0.00 m and 80 percent. The gap between P2 and
P3 is real and visible, which is presumably what was seen; P5 was simply not the comparison being
made. This is noted rather than smoothed over, because the eye reports rank badly and that is why
the measurement was taken first.

It is the model, and the cause is not what it looks like.

## What was measured

For all 120 passes of the demonstration pairing, three arc-length trajectories were extracted: the
decoder's own committed arc length straight out of the decoder, the rendered position as actually
drawn by the frame function the animation calls, and the labelled truth projected onto the line.
Nothing was changed first.

| | |
|---|---|
| decoder reaches within 0.5 m of the far end | **38.3 percent** of passes |
| the drawing reaches it | 33.3 percent |
| render-defect passes, decoder got there and the drawing did not | **7** |
| model-defect passes, the decoder itself never got there | **74** |
| median signed offset, red minus green, along the path | **+0.000 m** |

The last row matters on its own: the D1 render fix of 17 September holds, and no constant offset
has been reintroduced. What the operator is seeing is not an offset. It is the decoder stopping.

The stalls cluster tightly rather than scattering: the median stall sits at **0.80 of the way along
the line**, with the tenth and ninetieth percentiles at 0.62 and 0.85. A stall that clusters points
at something structural, and it does.

## The cause is the window budget, not the radio

The bounded decoder decodes only the windows labelled `walk`, the pass core. That is a median of
**4 windows out of the 10 labelled pass windows**, and a window's position label is at its CENTRE.
On a 5.00 m line those four centres sit at 0.50 m and 4.25 m and two points between. The first
0.50 m and the last 0.75 m of every walk are labelled `walk_edge`, walking without a position, and
are withheld from the decode.

**The decoder is not falling behind. It is not being asked.** Measured over the 72 straight-line
passes:

| | |
|---|---|
| truth at the decoder's first question | 0.50 m |
| decoder says, first question | **0.50 m** |
| truth at the decoder's last question | 4.25 m |
| decoder says, last question | **4.25 m** |
| furthest its speed model would have allowed | 4.39 m |
| line length | 5.00 m |

At its last question the decoder is **0.00 m** behind the person. It never reaches its own speed
cap on a single one of the 72 passes. The entire median shortfall of 0.75 m is walking that happens
after the last window it is given.

A correction to an earlier draft of this section, kept because the wrong version is instructive:
this was first written up as a speed-model limit, three transitions of V_MEAN reaching 3.9 m of a
5 m line. That arithmetic ignores that the decode starts at 0.50 m rather than at zero, and the
measured cruising speed of 1.250 m/s is if anything slower than the 1.298 m/s the model assumes, so
the transition model was never the binding constraint. The window COUNT still predicts the
shortfall at r = +0.807, because more windows means a last centre nearer the end of the line, but
the mechanism is coverage and not pace.

The prediction was tested against the measurement directly:

| predictor of the shortfall | correlation with the actual shortfall |
|---|---|
| window budget, `line length - (n windows - 1) x V_MEAN` | **r = +0.807** |
| emission strength on the true chain | **r = -0.025** |

Median absolute error of the window-budget prediction is 0.40 m.

## Why P3, which is the question that was asked

Not because the radio sees P3 worse. Per participant, ordered by how badly they stall:

| participant | emission strength on the true chain | walk windows per pass | decoder shortfall | tracked to end |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P3 | 1.430 | 4 of 10 | 1.32 m | 15 percent |
| P6 | 1.336 | 4 of 10 | 1.32 m | 20 percent |
| P4 | 1.522 | 4 of 10 | 1.00 m | 20 percent |
| P2 | 1.189 | 4 of 10 | 0.91 m | 35 percent |
| P1 | 1.134 | 5 of 11 | 0.50 m | 60 percent |
| P5 | 1.109 | 5 of 11 | 0.00 m | 80 percent |

The emission column runs the wrong way for the obvious explanation. The two participants the radio
sees **best**, P4 at 1.522 and P3 at 1.430, track worst. The two it sees **worst**, P5 at 1.109 and
P1 at 1.134, track best. What separates them is the middle column: P1 and P5 get five walk windows
per pass and everyone else gets four. A participant whose walk spans one more window gets one more
transition, which is 1.298 m more reach.

So the honest answer to "why P3" is that P3 walks the line quickly enough to span four windows
rather than five. Stalling is a symptom of walking fast, not of being hard to see.

## The fix, and why it does not ship

Forced alignment was implemented as specified: decode the whole declared pass rather than only its
core, with the chain's first cell pinned at the declared start time and its last cell at the
declared end time. Standard forced Viterbi, no new parameters, and it uses only the start time and
end time that the bounded mode is already granted. It is a flag on the bounded decoder, default
off, so the live unbounded decoder is untouched.

Three bars were set before it was run. It clears two.

| bar | result | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| stall fraction falls to near zero | 36.7 to **100.0 percent** tracked to end, stall fraction **0.0** | PASS |

And the cost that decides it: **line identification collapses from 65.0 percent to 29.2 percent**,
worse on 9 of 9 cross-day pairings. **The fix does not ship.**

The pinned decoder is not simply gliding: it beats constant-speed dead reckoning on every pairing,

it does when the decoder is asked only about the pass core.

## The ablation, which is the finding worth keeping

Pinning changes two things at once: it widens the decode to the whole declared pass, and it pins
both ends. A variant that does only the first, widening the window with no pin at all, was run on
the same passes:

| | line identification | median error when right | tracked to end |
|---|---|---|---|
| decode the walk core only, as shipped | **65.0 percent** | **0.323 m** | 36.7 percent |
| widen to the whole declared pass, no pin | 29.2 percent | 0.815 m | 100.0 percent |
| widen and pin both ends | 29.2 percent | 0.815 m | 100.0 percent |

The two lower rows are the same to the precision reported. **The terminal pin costs nothing on its
own; the widening costs everything**: 35.8 line points and 0.49 m.

That inverts the obvious reading of the defect. The restriction to the pass core is not an
oversight that happens to cause a stall. It is load-bearing. The `walk_edge` windows are the run-in
and run-out, walking without a position, and admitting them to the decode drags the Viterbi onto
the wrong chain more than twice as often. The same four windows that cannot reach the end of the
line are what keep the line decision right.

**So the stall is the price of the accuracy, and it is worth paying.** A decoder that always
reaches the end of the line and names the right line 29 percent of the time is a worse instrument
than one that stops 1 m short and names the right line 65 percent of the time.

One further check, because it is the obvious worry. If stalling were a symptom of the tracker
struggling on a pass, then stalled passes should also be the ones it gets wrong. On the
demonstration pairing they are not:

| | passes | line named correctly |
|---|---|---|
| tracked to the far end | 46 | 76.1 percent |
| stopped short | 74 | **82.4 percent** |

The passes where the dot stops short are, if anything, the ones the tracker gets right slightly
more often. The visible defect is not a symptom of a bad decode. It is the window budget, and it is
close to independent of whether the decode was any good.

## What did change: the drawing now tells the truth about it

Since the decoder genuinely stops short, the render's job is to show that rather than blur it. Two
changes, both making the drawing more faithful to the decoder and neither touching the model:

- the dot is never drawn past the arc length the decoder actually committed to. The previous cap
  allowed a full extra window of travel beyond the last decision.
- when a committed run ends, the dot holds at that last committed arc length for the rest of the
  declared walk instead of vanishing to the room centre mid-stride. A dot that disappears and a dot
  that stops look different on screen, and only one of them is what happened.

Measured on the same 120 passes, before and after, as medians per pass:

| | previous drawing | shipping drawing |
|---|---|---|
| red drawn at the room centre while the person is still walking | 0.30 s | **0.00 s** |
| red holding on the line while the person is still walking | 1.00 s | 1.30 s |
| render reaches the far end | 33.3 percent | 34.2 percent |

The first row is the whole point. Previously the dot vanished to the middle of the room for about
a third of a second in the middle of every walk, which on screen reads as the tracker guessing
rather than as the tracker having stopped. It now never does that: it stays where the decoder
last committed, for as long as the declared walk lasts.

`TDR_RENDER_LEGACY=1` reproduces the previous drawing exactly, which is what the phase 5.1
reproduction test uses.

This fix was itself wrong on the first attempt and the measurement is what caught it. The hold was
keyed by the last labelled window of the pass, while the drawing's own runs end at the last
walk-mode window, so the lookup never matched and the hold never fired. The rendered output looked
plausible and the centre-time metric did not move. Keying it by the run's own end index is what
took that row from 0.30 s to 0.00 s.

**What to look for in P3's video.** The red dot now advances cleanly to about four fifths of the
line and stops there, holding, while green walks on to the 5 m mark. That is the decoder running
out of windows, and it is the honest picture of a real limitation rather than a rendering artefact.

And the thing worth knowing before watching it: **P3's recording names the right line on 17 of 20
passes** while reaching the far end on 3 of 20. It is simultaneously the worst-looking video in the
set and one of the most accurate. That single pair of numbers is the whole finding, and it is why
the per-pass table sits beside the videos in `runs/constrained/demo/ACCEPTANCE.md`: the eye reads
the gap, and the gap is not the result.

## What would actually fix it

Not a decoder change. The window budget is set by the labelling: a pass gets `walk` windows only
where a position label exists, and at a 1 s stride a 4 s walk yields four of them. Two levers exist
and both belong to the second collection rather than to this code:

- **a shorter stride.** At 0.5 s the same 4 s walk yields eight windows and the arithmetic
  disappears. The 0.5 s arm exists and is reported elsewhere in this document; it was not adopted
  for other reasons, and this is a further argument for revisiting it.
- **deliberate speed variation**, already the first item on the second-collection protocol sheet
  for the identification question. It is also the direct test of this one: if the shortfall is the
  window budget, then asking the same participant to walk the same line slowly should move them
  from four windows to six and close the gap without any change to the model.

# The stall, part two: the split decode, and a second defect

Added 2026-09-18 after the operator rejected the first write-up on three specific grounds. He was
right on all three, and two of them were errors of mine.

## The label cut-off is not universal, and P3 stops before its own

The first section reported a pooled lag of 0.00 m between where the person was at the decoder's
last question and how far the decoder had got, and concluded the decoder was simply never asked
about the rest. That pooled figure was the median of two opposing groups cancelling.

Per participant, straight 5.00 m lines, medians:

| participant | label cut-off | decoder stops | residual lag | same on diagonals |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P3 | 4.00 m | 4.00 m | **+0.38 m** | **+0.50 m** |
| P6 | 4.12 m | 4.00 m | **+0.38 m** | **+0.62 m** |
| P4 | 4.38 m | 4.12 m | **+0.50 m** | **+0.62 m** |
| P2 | 4.25 m | 4.00 m | +0.00 m | +0.12 m |
| P1 | 4.25 m | 4.38 m | +0.00 m | −0.32 m |
| P5 | 4.50 m | 5.00 m | −0.38 m | −0.25 m |

Two separate things are going on. The cut-off itself varies from 4.00 m to 4.50 m, because a
faster walker's pass spans fewer windows and the last window centre therefore falls earlier. That
is the coverage effect and it is the larger one. But on top of it, **P3, P4 and P6 stop short of
their own cut-off** while P1 and P5 run past it. That residual is a second defect and it needed
its own diagnosis.

It is not explained by any of the obvious candidates: correlation with emission strength is
**+0.044**, with line length **+0.168**, with window count **−0.202**. On 16 percent of passes the
decoder is more than 0.5 m behind its own last question. The cause of the residual is **not
established**, and it is recorded here as open rather than explained away. What can be said is
that the intervention below removes it in practice, which is consistent with too few windows to
advance through but does not prove it.

## The split decode

The two questions the bounded decoder is really being asked are separable. WHICH LINE is a
classification over the twelve chains; WHERE ALONG IT is a position estimate given that answer.
The earlier ablation showed that widening the decode window costs 35.8 line points, but it showed
that for a decode in which the extra windows could still change the line. If the line is already
fixed, they cannot.

So: choose the line from the pass core exactly as before, then extend the track outwards through
the ramp windows along the chosen chain.

**One false start, kept because it is the instructive part.** The first implementation re-decoded
the whole declared walk jointly along the chosen chain. Line identification was indeed untouched,
and coverage went to 100 percent, but median absolute arc error on the core windows nearly tripled,
0.250 m to 0.750 m. The cause is that the cruising speed prior does not hold across a span that
includes standing still at both ends: applied to the whole declared walk it wants 11.68 m of travel
on a 5.00 m line, 2.3 times the line, so it saturates at the far end early and drags the core with
it. Swapping to a broad prior centred on zero displacement fails in the opposite direction, ending
within 0.5 m of the line end on 4.5 percent of passes.

The version that ships does not re-decode the core at all. It pins the core to what it already is
and fills in the ramp windows only, forwards from the last core state and backwards from the first.
Line identification and position error on the labelled windows are then unchanged **by
construction** rather than by measurement.

Over all nine cross-day pairings, 1080 passes:

| | shipped before | split decode |
|---|---|---|
| line identification | 63.241 percent | **63.241 percent**, identical on 1080 of 1080 |
| median error when right | 0.308 m | **0.308 m**, identical to 1e-9 on all 683 |
| core states | | **identical on 1080 of 1080** |
| coverage of the line | 85.00 percent | **100.00 percent** |
| reaches within 0.5 m of the end | 36.39 percent | **87.41 percent** |

Coverage rises and nothing else moves. That is what the ship rule asked for, and on that rule it
ships. **But the coverage it buys is not tracking, and the next section is the reason.**

On the demonstration pairing the dot now reaches the far end on **92.5 percent** of passes against
38.3 before, and every participant's median shortfall is **0.00 m**, including the three that had a
residual lag:

| participant | reaches within 0.5 m, before | after |
|---|---|---|
| P3 | 15 percent | **95 percent** |
| P6 | 20 percent | **90 percent** |
| P4 | 20 percent | **85 percent** |
| P2 | 35 percent | **95 percent** |
| P1 | 60 percent | **95 percent** |
| P5 | 80 percent | **95 percent** |

## THE RAMP EXTENSION IS DEAD RECKONING, AND IT IS NOT TRACKING

The operator asked the right question before accepting any of this: is the model working from the
signals, or is it pacing. It is pacing, on the ramp windows, and here is the measurement.

For each of the 340 ramp windows the extension fills in, the decision was recomputed three ways:

| what was changed | ramp windows where the answer changed |
|---|---|
| the CSI emissions removed entirely | **0.0 percent** |
| a different participant's CSI substituted | **0.6 percent** |
| the transition prior removed instead | 74.4 percent |

**Removing the radio changes nothing.** The transition prior decides every ramp window on its own.
Widening that prior to let the emissions compete does not rescue it: at sixteen times the fitted
standard deviation the radio still moves only 3.8 percent of those windows.

The reason is in the label definition rather than in the code. `walk_edge` means walking WITHOUT a
position: the person is starting or stopping, and the emission model was never trained on such
windows. They carry almost no positional information, so there is nothing for the decoder to read
and the prior is all that is left.

So the honest statement is: **the ramp extension advances the dot at the model's assumed walking
speed, not at a speed read from the radio.** It is the same kind of object as the dashed
extrapolation, and it is now drawn the same way: dashed, with a hollow dot, and captioned
"inferred from the given start and end, NOT from the radio".

### What this means for every number in this section

- **Coverage of 100 percent and reaches-the-end of 87 to 92 percent are NOT tracking results.**
  They are what the dot draws once inference is allowed. They must never be quoted as decoder
  performance.
- **The decoder's own signal-driven coverage is unchanged at 85 percent of the line**, which is
  where the radio stops being informative. That is the honest figure.
- **The headline is untouched and remains the only claim worth making: 63.2 percent of lines named
  correctly cross-day at 0.308 m median error when right.**

### The core, by contrast, is genuinely driven by the radio

The same substitution test on the pass-core windows, which are the ones every quoted number comes
from:

| | |
|---|---|
| same LINE chosen from another participant's CSI | **16.7 percent**, against 8.3 percent chance |
| same POSITION state chosen from another participant's CSI | **25.6 percent** |

Swapping the radio changes the line on five passes in six and the position on three windows in
four. The tracker proper reads the signals. It is only the cosmetic completion of the line that
does not, and it is now labelled as such wherever it appears.

It is worth saying plainly that the earlier `pin_end` result stands as a separate finding. Letting
the extra windows change the LINE costs 35.8 points. Letting them only extend the POSITION costs
nothing. The distinction is the whole content of this section.

## The renderer

The inferred segment is drawn **dashed, with a hollow dot**, and the caption now reads
"GIVEN: when each walk starts and ends. NOT GIVEN: which of the twelve lines. DASHED + HOLLOW:
inferred, not measured." It carries the dot from the decoder's last committed position to the end
of the line over the declared time remaining, which is inference from the boundaries the mode is
already granted and not anything the radio said. With the split decode shipping it is usually
nothing at all, which is the point.

# The ends of the line

Added 2026-09-18. The bounded tracker stops short of the far end of each line. This section is the
attempt to make those ends MEASURED rather than inferred, and the measured negative it produced.

## Why the end windows were unlabelled

A window's truth position comes from interpolation between the pass anchor times. A window whose
CENTRE falls outside that interval has no interval to interpolate within, so it is tagged
`walk_edge`, walking without a position. At the 1 s stride that is a median of three windows before
the first anchor press and three after the last, against four that carry a label.

## The labelling rule, and its bound

The protocol fixes what interpolation cannot. A pass is DEFINED by torso crossings of the taped
start and end of the line, so the participant is standing at the start tape before the first press
and at the end tape after the last. Those windows can therefore be labelled from the path geometry
with no interpolation at all.

Bounded to **1.0 s either side**, because further out the person may be walking to or from the
start position and the label would be a guess. That bound admits **one window at each end**, not
three: **702 windows in total**, two per pass, a 50 percent increase in labelled windows.

Label uncertainty at a tape is **0.10 m** from the grid sweep, against **0.20 m** for a position
interpolated between two presses while the person is moving. **The end labels are the cleaner
ones**, which is the whole argument for using them.

## The retrain, and the like-for-like check that stopped it

The heads were retrained on the extended label set in an isolated artefact tree, same recipe, same
seed, same epoch rule, changing nothing but the labels.

**The decision was made on the aggregate and not on the demonstration pairing.** `s0102_to_s03` is
one of the nine cross-day pairings; choosing on it is test-side selection, a class this project's
audit already records five times. Compared like for like on the ORIGINAL core windows, cluster
bootstrap over passes, 1000 resamples, seed 0:

| | frozen heads | extended-label heads |
|---|---|---|
| aggregate line identification, median over 9 cross-day pairings | 65.0 percent | 68.3 percent |
| pairings won | **6** | 3 |
| pairings where the interval excludes zero | **3** | **0** |

The three significant pairings all favour the frozen heads: `s0203_to_s01` −15.0 [−22.5, −7.5],
`s0103_to_s02` −11.7 [−19.2, −4.1], `s0102_to_s03` −11.7 [−19.2, −4.2]. **The gain is outside the
interval on no pairing at all.**

**The apparent 63.2 to 70.8 gain was an artefact of comparing unlike things.** 70.8 came from the
new heads WITH the end windows decoded; 63.2 from the frozen heads without. On the same windows the
aggregate median moves 65.0 to 68.3, and the intervals say that is not a gain.

**The demonstration-pairing figure, reported and not used to decide:** 80.0 to 68.3 percent, delta
−11.7 with interval [−19.2, −4.2], which excludes zero. The loss is real. The decision is identical
without it.

**THE STOP CONDITION FIRED.** Cell accuracy on the core moved by more than 0.05 on four pairings:
0.190 to 0.266, 0.156 to 0.103, 0.434 to 0.379, 0.368 to 0.279. The extended labels changed what
the heads learned about the middle of the line. Position error alone moved beyond the 0.11 m floor
on **no** pairing, which is why the phase 2 check that looked only at position error missed it.

**The frozen heads ship. The retrain is reported as a measured negative.**

# The live mode and the stride

`runs/TRACKING_FROZEN_2026-09-18f.json`. Every figure here is cross-day over nine pairings unless

## The three modes, with the stride and chance on every row

The demonstration ships at a **0.5 s stride** by decision O3. The unbounded figure is a 1 s figure
and is labelled as one, because putting it beside two half-second figures without saying so is how
a table misleads.

| told | mode | stride | line identification | against chance |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| nothing | unbounded, live | 1 s | 14.4 percent | 1.7 times |
| **when a walk STARTS** | **semi-bounded, LIVE** | **0.5 s** | **46.7 percent** | **5.6 times** |
| when it starts AND ends | bounded, retrospective | 0.5 s | 58.3 percent | 7.0 times |
| when it starts AND ends | bounded, retrospective | 1 s | 65.0 percent | 7.8 times |
| chance, twelve lines | | | 8.3 percent | |

**THE LIVE RESULT IS 46.7 PERCENT AT FIVE AND A HALF TIMES CHANCE, AND THAT IS THE BRIEF'S
REAL-TIME REQUIREMENT MET AND MEASURED.** Semi-bounded is told only when a walk begins, which is a
fact available at the moment it happens. Bounded is told when the walk ENDS, which is a future
fact, so bounded is a retrospective upper bound on what the radio contains rather than a system
that could run. The honest headline for a live device is the 46.7, not the 58.3 or the 65.0, and it
is stated that way rather than only as a mode that missed a bar.

The 46.7 figure is the best arm that PASSES the end-time substitution gate. It is not the highest
line figure measured; see the gate section below.

## The commitment timeline, and what it actually shows

At 1, 2, 3 and 4 seconds after the start press, the line a viewer is being SHOWN is the correct one
on **12.5, 19.2, 28.3 and 39.2 percent** of passes.

**That is not a failure, and it is not a display bug. A pass takes about four seconds, so the line
is still assembling at the moment the walk ends.** The system does not converge during the walk at
all: the final answer of 46.7 percent is produced by the whole pass, rescored at the decoder's own
end, and not by the display settling on an answer partway through.

That settles a question this project went back and forth on. A fixed display hold of a few tenths
of a second was never going to be enough, because at four seconds the display is still wrong on
three passes in five. And a confidence gate, which would show nothing until the estimate was good
enough, would spend most of every walk showing nothing. Neither is a tuning problem. The evidence
arrives across the whole pass, so any display that commits early commits on less than half of it.

## D1: the decoder tracks walking pace, and the end was the wrong thing to blame

The decoder's implied traversal speed, arc covered over wall-clock elapsed, is a median **1.26 m/s
at 1 s and 1.43 m/s at 0.5 s**, with **no pass above 2.0 m/s** at either stride. The true walking
speed over the position-labelled span of the same passes is **1.30 m/s**, the 1.302 reference. The
motion model is right and the decoder obeys it.

The reported "exits 2.0 s early" was an artefact of what it was scored against. That figure used
`t0[p_hi] + WINDOW_S`, the far edge of the pass span; `recordings.py` documents those windows as a
run-out grace zone within 3 s of the pass in which the person IS still walking. Measured against
the END ANCHOR PRESS, the physical event and the same instrument the start press uses, the decoder
ends **+1.33 s LATE at 1 s** and **−0.10 s at 0.5 s**. The reaction-time bias of 51 ms is already
subtracted at labelling time in every `.gt.json` and must not be subtracted twice.

The three candidate definitions of the end sit a long way apart, and all three are reported on
every row so the choice is auditable: the pass-span end is **+3.25 s** after the press, and the
last position-labelled window centre is **−0.68 s** before it, which is about 0.88 m of line at
walking pace.

## Decomposing the gap, and the two measured nulls

At 1 s: the shipped rule, which takes a majority over a 20 s horizon, scores 28.3 percent; the same
decode confined to the true walk scores 64.2; all twelve chains kept alive scores 60.8; the bounded
global decode scores 65.0. **Confining the decode to the walk is worth 35.8 points and commitment
is worth −3.3.** The gap was the end, not the decoder's inability to change its mind.

Three constraints were built to fix the end and **all three measured inert**, which is recorded as
a result rather than hidden:

- an **arc-fraction null lockout** cannot bind, because the decoder already reaches a median 95.5
  percent of its chain before leaving
- a **maximum-speed cap** at 1.6, 2.0 or 2.4 m/s cannot bind on a decoder whose p90 implied speed
  is 1.27 m/s
- a **time-based null lockout** of T = chain length over 1.8 to 2.6 m/s gives T ≈ 1.9 to 2.8 s and
  cannot bind on a traversal that takes 4.0 s

Every arm containing them is numerically identical to the arm without, and every grid value ties,
so each is recorded as NOT SELECTED rather than chosen: a constraint that cannot bind has not been
selected by anything.

## The exit sweep, and a defect in a rule's wording

The sweep was first written to make the decoder leave LATER and was reversed once the end was
scored against the press, because the decoder ends late rather than early. Run in the correct
direction, raising `exit_p` and `exit_k` to permit an earlier exit, **it changes nothing**: the
aggregate line is 53.3 percent before and after and the end against the press moves 1.337 to 1.337
seconds. `exit_p` was never selected away from its shipped 0.5 on any pairing. **The sweep ships
nothing and the shipped exit values stay.**

The brief's rule, "beats the shipped values outside the interval on more than one pairing", fired
on the first run and is **a defect in the rule rather than a result**. It was written for comparing
ARMS, where one arm ships everywhere; this phase selects a value PER PAIRING, and nine pairings
choosing nine values is nine configurations while a deployed system has one. It needs an aggregate
condition to mean anything, and the same wording must not be reused for parameter selection without
one.

**EVERY LEVER AVAILABLE AT THE ONE-SECOND STRIDE HAS NOW BEEN TRIED AND THE END IS NOT FIXABLE
THERE.** That is what sent the batch to the stride.

## The stride: it buys the end and costs the line

Bounded, cross-day, nine pairings:

| | 1 s | 0.5 s |
|---|---|---|
| line identification | 65.0 percent | 58.3 percent |
| median error when right | 0.323 m | **0.213 m**, better outside the interval on **9 of 9** |
| coverage of the line by the decoder's own positions | 85 percent | **100 percent** |
| passes reaching within 0.5 m of the line end | 36.7 percent | **90.0 percent** |
| end-window substitution | 95.8 percent | **90.8 percent**, against a 93.3 percent core |

**The coverage figure STANDS because it survives the substitution test.** At 0.5 s the end windows
carry labels, the heads have seen them, and swapping another participant's CSI changes the decoded
end position on 90.8 percent of passes against 93.3 for the core. That is the test the ramp
extension failed at 0.0 percent on the morning of 2026-09-18, and it is why coverage is quoted here
at all.

**The mechanism is labelling, not resolution.** At 1 s the last 0.68 s of every line carries no
labelled window, so the emission model had never seen a line ending and had nothing to read there.
The 0.5 s stride labels exactly those windows. The same change shows up everywhere the end matters:
end-time substitution 53.3 to 80.0 percent, end error against the press +1.33 to −0.10 s, and
`rescore_end` going from −9.2 points to 0.0 alone and +1.67 with the time lockout, because
rescoring at a correctly detected end is no longer rescoring over a window that stops short.

### O3, an operator override

The pre-registered ship rule for the stride has four conditions. Three pass: error better outside
the interval on more than one pairing, coverage rises, and the end-window substitution test passes.
One fails: line identification is not inside the band on the aggregate. **The rule FAILED and that
result stands.** The operator overrode it on 2026-09-18 and it is recorded as an OVERRIDE, not as a
reinterpretation of the wording, because recording it the other way would hide a decision behind a
definition. The precedent is **O1**, the bout-decoder override of 2026-09-17 14:35.

**Both readings of the failing axis are printed permanently and the override rests on neither.**
Median-of-rates, the difference of the two medians, is **−6.7 points**; the median of the
per-pairing PAIRED deltas, computed pass by pass on `stem|pass_id`, is **0.0 points**, with two
pairings up outside their intervals and one down. They disagree because the median selects a
different pairing on each side, so median-of-rates is not a paired comparison at all. **That
ambiguity is the second defect in a rule's wording found in this batch**: the project's other ship
rules are phrased per pairing, so "the aggregate" had no settled meaning when the rule was written,
and any future rule using the phrase must say which quantity it means.

On the demonstration pairing the line cost is 80.0 to 78.3 percent, which is two passes in 120.

## THE BATCH'S MAIN FINDING: the same defect twice in one day, in two disguises

On the morning of 2026-09-18 the **ramp extension** was deleted: it read the CSI in its own
expression and ignored it in practice, and removing the emissions changed **0.0 percent** of its
answers. It was a segment DRAWN without evidence.

On the evening of the same day **pinned entry** was refused by the ship rule for the same reason in
a different place. It is an end INFERRED from the clock.

**The mechanism.** Pinning entry fixes the decoder's position at a known state at a known time, and
the chain carries a single sharp global duration prior. Arrival at the end of the chain therefore
becomes partly a function of ELAPSED TIME rather than of the emissions, and another participant's
CSI moves the end less often because less of the end is being read.

**The two numbers.** Pinned entry's marginal effect on end-time substitution is **−12.5 points at
1 s** and **−32.5 points at 0.5 s**, against **0.00 for `all_alive` and `rescore_end`** at both
strides. Every arm containing pinned entry fails the gate at both strides. It bought 9.2 and 7.5
points of line identification and the gate took all of it back.

**Neither disguise was caught by code review, by a coverage figure or by an exit code. Both were
caught by a test that did not exist beforehand**: the substitution test in the morning, and the
end-time substitution gate in the evening.

### The guard is a gate, not an axis

An arm whose end-time substitution falls at or below 50 percent is OUT, whatever its line figure;
among the arms that pass, take the best line. Applied in that order:

| stride | best arm overall | best GUARD-PASSING arm |
|---|---|---|
| 1 s | `pinned_entry+all_alive` 53.3 percent, substitution 40.8, **FAILS** | 44.2 percent, substitution 53.3 |
| 0.5 s | `pinned_entry` 50.0 percent, substitution 47.5, **FAILS** | **46.7 percent**, substitution 76.7 |

Applying it the other way round, taking the best line and consulting the guard afterwards, would
have shipped a 53.3 percent figure whose end is partly a clock. The gate costs 9.2 points of line
at 1 s and 3.3 at 0.5 s, and those points are the price of an end that is measured.

## The verdict

**SEMI-BOUNDED DOES NOT BECOME THE CORE MODE.** The shippable figure is 46.7 percent against
bounded's 58.3 at the same stride, a gap of **11.6 points** against a **ten-point** bar. No bar was
reworded.

The verdict was recorded in `sb3v_verdict` **before phase 4 produced the bounded figure it is
measured against**, and that ordering is asserted rather than claimed: the stage checks that no
phase 4 results exist and recorded `taken_before_phase4: True`. A later re-run refuses to overwrite
a verdict taken in that order.

## NO DECISION IN THIS PROJECT IS TAKEN ON A SINGLE PAIRING

This is now a rule, and it was earned three times on 2026-09-18:

1. the **exit sweep** showed four intervals excluding zero, and had **none** once passes were
   paired on `stem|pass_id` instead of `pass_id`
2. the **heads decision** was deliberately taken on nine pairings rather than on the demonstration
   pairing, which pointed the other way
3. **pinned entry** RAISED the end-time guard by 15.8 points on `s01_to_s02` while costing 12.5 at
   1 s and 32.5 at 0.5 s on the aggregate: the opposite sign

## The pairing-key audit

A comparison keyed on `pass_id` alone collapses six recordings into one dict of twenty entries,
because `pass_id` restarts at 1 in every recording. Eleven sites were audited. Ten were safe,
per-recording and consumed inside their own loop. One was not: the exit sweep's paired difference,
which fed a bootstrap interval which fed a ship rule. Corrected, its four significant intervals
became zero.

**The heads decision is NOT affected**: `re6_heads` keys its comparison rows `stem|pass_id` and
pairs on their intersection. **No substitution rate quoted today is affected**: both substitution
sites build and consume their dicts inside one recording's loop. `tests/test_pass_pairing_key.py`
now fails on any unclassified `pass_id`-keyed dict, and a planted regression was confirmed caught.

## Future work, not run

**A guard-safe pinned entry.** Pinning the start is only a clock because the duration prior is
SHARP. Flattening that prior inside a declared walk may keep the geometric gain of entering at the
known first state without making arrival at the chain's end time-determined. Its motivation is the
two marginals, −12.5 and −32.5 points of end-time substitution, against the 9.2 and 7.5 points of
line identification it bought.

**A re-sweep of the exit parameters is NOT future work.** It was run in the correct direction and
changed nothing.

# The window and the gate

A probe, run 2026-09-18 into 2026-09-19 on branch `window-probe-2026-09-18`. **Nothing in it ships,
no freeze was written, and the demonstration renders are unchanged.** Its output is a
recommendation about whether to train eleven more pairings, and it is ONE pairing and ONE seed.

## Feasibility, which the probe depended on

**The shipped encoder accepts a shorter window with no architectural change.** `LinkCNN` holds
79,600 parameters at 300, 150 and 75 samples alike, because an `AdaptiveAvgPool2d` before the
embedding makes the flattened dimension independent of the time axis. The embedding also RESPONDS
to its input, so "accepted" means read rather than tolerated. The probe is therefore the same
experiment with a shorter input, not a different network wearing the same name.

**The motion gate threshold IS window-length dependent, and it was re-derived rather than retuned.**
`band_energy` integrates FFT bins between 0.3 and 2 Hz and the bin spacing is `fs/n_samples`, so the
band holds four bins at 2 s, two at 1 s and one at 0.5 s. The shipped 0.431 was measured on the
four-bin statistic and does not transfer. Re-derived at the same quantile it becomes 0.0507 at 1 s
and 0.0092 at 0.5 s, and **walking recall is 100 percent at both**, so the veto's safety property
holds and the gate is trusted.

**The labelled-window count does not move with the window length.** It is 9.0 per pass at every
window, because the count is set by the STRIDE and the pass duration. The brief expected it to rise
and it does not; what the shorter window changes is how much of each window lies outside the
anchors, which is measured below by substitution rather than predicted.

## The confidence gate: there is no usable margin

Confidence is the MARGIN between the leading chain and the runner-up in the decoder's own
log-likelihood units, never a percentage. Swept over eight values drawn from the observed
distribution, on the shipped configuration over nine cross-day pairings:

| margin | commits on | median time to commit | correct when it commits |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.00 | 100.0 percent | 1.0 s | 10.8 percent |
| 2.04 | 95.8 percent | 3.5 s | 34.5 percent |
| 4.07 | 80.0 percent | 5.0 s | 43.7 percent |
| 6.11 | 45.0 percent | 5.5 s | 51.5 percent |
| 7.12 | 33.3 percent | 5.5 s | 59.3 percent |

**No margin exists at which the display commits on more than half of passes before the walk ends
AND is right more often than not when it does.** The first margin whose commitments are right more
often than not commits at a median 5.5 s, on a walk lasting about four. Every margin either shows
nothing for most of the walk or shows something wrong more often than right. That is the MEASURED
version of the inference recorded in the semi-bounded batch, and it closes the question: a
confidence gate is not a tuning problem here.

## The shorter window: it does not make the line appear sooner

Commitment timeline on `s0102_to_s03`, the same eight points and the same code path, differing only
in the window:

| t | 2 s (shipped) | 1 s | 0.5 s |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.0 s | 8.3 | 9.2 | 8.3 |
| 2.0 s | **55.0** | 32.5 | 40.0 |
| 3.0 s | **68.6** | 51.7 | 60.0 |
| 4.0 s | **72.8** | 67.2 | 73.3 |

**The shipped 2 s window dominates almost everywhere.** It crosses the 39.2 percent deciding line
at **1.70 s**; the 1 s window at **2.31 s** and the 0.5 s window at **1.98 s**, which is 0.61 and
0.27 seconds LATER. The verdict's timing bar required at least a second EARLIER, so both arms fail
it and the 2 s window stands.

**What the shorter window does not cost is accuracy.** Bounded line identification is 79.2 percent
at 1 s and 80.0 at 0.5 s against the shipped 78.3 on this pairing; coverage is 100 percent at both;
the dot reaches the end on 93.3 percent against 91.7. End-window substitution is 88.3 percent
against a core of about 91, so the ends are measured at both windows and the coverage figures
stand. Semi-bounded keeps its guard: end-time substitution 83.3 and 79.2 percent, both above the
bar, with zero impossible transitions on every committed track.

So the finding is narrow and clean: **a shorter analysis window is affordable and harmless, and it
does not buy latency.** Three of the four verdict conditions pass at both windows and the timing
condition fails at both. NOT RECOMMENDED for the other eleven pairings.

## A THIRD instance of comparing unlike things, caught before it decided anything

The deciding number was first computed against **39.2 percent at 4.0 s**, which is where the
NINE-PAIRING MEDIAN arrives. Measured that way the 1 s window looked 1.69 s EARLIER and would have
been recommended. But 39.2 is an aggregate and the arm is one pairing: against this pairing's OWN
2 s curve the same arm is 0.61 s LATER. **The verdict reversed entirely on the choice of baseline.**

That is the third time in this project that a single pairing has been measured against an aggregate,
after the 70.8 percent bounded figure taken from heads that never shipped and the exit sweep paired
on `pass_id` alone. The baseline is now this pairing's own curve, carried in the results as
`baseline_is`, with the aggregate kept beside it labelled `aggregate_reference_only` and a note
saying why it is not used.

## What the feasibility phase missed, and the lesson

Phase 0 reported that NONE of the six window-quantised parameters depends on the window length. That
was true of the six and it was the wrong claim to make, because **the audit enumerated the brief's
list rather than the code**. Training then failed on `NS = 300` in `recordings.py`, a hardcoded
300-sample RSSI lattice concatenated with a 150-sample CSI window, described in plain words in two
docstrings as "each window's own 300-sample lattice". The gate threshold was caught only because
the brief asked about it.

`rssi_series` now takes an `n_samples` argument defaulting to `NS`, and `load_rssi` derives the
lattice from the recording's own window metadata, so the shipped path is bit-identical and verified
so. A latent bug beside it was fixed at the same time: the RSSI cache validated window count and
timestamps but NOT the sample count, so a series cached at one lattice would have been served for
another.

**A feasibility phase that checks only what it was handed is a checklist.** The next one greps for
the dependency instead of enumerating the ones somebody remembered.

# The live mode, second pass

An overnight batch, 2026-09-19, branch `semibounded-night-2026-09-19`. Frozen at
`runs/TRACKING_FROZEN_2026-09-18h.json`. **The verdict is unchanged and the shipped figure did not
move: semi-bounded stays at 46.7 percent and does not become the core mode.** What the night bought
is not a number but a set of closed questions, and one of them closes the state document's only
live idea.

## The diagnosis, which was encouraging and is where the night's one real lesson sits

Splitting every pass by whether the decoder's end landed within a second of the anchor press:

| | median over pairings |
|---|---|
| end within a second of the press | **73.4 percent** |
| end off by more than a second | 21.1 percent |
| the same decode over the TRUE walk window | 58.3 percent |

**Exactly half the passes, 49.1 percent, have a good end.** So the whole eleven-point gap, and more,
sits in end quality, and the decode itself is not the problem: on passes where the interval is
right, semi-bounded scores well ABOVE bounded's 58.3.

**That 73.4 is conditional and it is not a target.** End quality is not assigned at random. The
decoder gets the end right on the passes where the signal is clearest, which are the same passes it
would decode well anyway, so 73.4 is an upper bound on what fixing the end could buy and probably an
inflated one. It is recorded here as a diagnosis, never as a headline.

The tail is **85 percent stops-short and 15 percent overshoots**, which is the opposite of what the
arm designs assumed. Reverse confusions are only **5.5 percent** of wrong decisions, so the original
motivation for pinned entry, that it fixes a reverse-line problem, is much weaker than it looked.

## What was tried, and what the guard refused

Thirteen single-lever arms, every one gated on the substitution test before its line figure counts.
The baseline reproduced the shipped decoder to four decimals, 71.667 against 71.7 on the
demonstration pairing, so the arms were measured against the right thing.

| arm | line | end substitution | guard |
|---|---|---|---|
| **hard pinned entry, sharp prior** | **48.3** | **47.5** | **FAILS** |
| per-chain prior correction | 45.8 | 80.0 | passes |
| likelihood rule, family-first | 45.0 | 80.0 | passes |
| shipped | 42.5 | 80.0 | passes |
| pinned entry, prior flattened 0.5 / 0.9 | 25.8 / 27.5 | 21.7 / 20.8 | FAILS |
| candidate-end rescore | 24 to 28 | 80.0 | passes |

**THE GUARD-SAFE PINNED ENTRY IDEA IS DEAD, and the way it died is the finding.** The hypothesis was
that pinning only behaved like a clock because the duration prior was sharp, so flattening the prior
should keep the geometric gain without making arrival time-determined. It does not.
**Flattening makes both things worse at once**: the guard falls from 47.5 to 21.7 percent and the
line falls from 48.3 to 25.8. Sharpness was never the mechanism. Whatever makes a pinned start
behave like a clock is not the prior's confidence about how long a cell takes, and the state
document's one live idea is closed.

**The candidate-end rescore backfired**, 24 to 28 percent against 42.5. Attacking the end directly
made things much worse even though the end is where the whole gap lives. That is unexplained and it
is recorded as unexplained.

## What survived, and why it is not enough

Three arms passed the guard and beat the shipped line, and all three are unglamorous: a per-chain
prior correction fitted on the training sessions, taking the final line by total chain likelihood
rather than by majority vote over a free Viterbi, and deciding the family before the member.

**They barely stack.** Marginals on top of each other: per-chain prior **+1.7**, family-first
**+0.8**, likelihood rule **0.0**. Together they give 46.7 percent against the no-arm 42.5, which
lands exactly on the figure the mode already had.

One of them is worth keeping for a reason other than its line figure: the per-chain prior raises
**line substitution to 91.7 percent** against the shipped 85.8, so the decision is more
signal-driven, not less.

## The verdict

**SEMI-BOUNDED DOES NOT BECOME THE CORE MODE.** The shipped configuration, the three surviving arms
together, scores **46.7 percent** against bounded's **58.3** at the same stride: a gap of **11.7
points against a ten-point bar**, and outside the five-point stretch as well.

It passes three of the four conditions. Median end error is inside a second, the never-ended
fraction is under five percent, and both substitutions pass. It fails only on the gap.

The verdict was taken **before phase 6 rendered or froze anything**, and the ordering is asserted
rather than claimed: the stage checked that phase 6 had written no results and recorded
`taken_before_renders: True`. **The demonstration renders are unchanged** and the bounded set stands
exactly as it was.

## The calibration probe, labelled, deciding nothing

Day-only calibration on `s0102_to_s03` only, each participant calibrating in turn and the other five
scored: **81.5 percent calibrated against 81.5 uncalibrated, a difference of 0.0 points** over six
calibrators. On this pairing, with this configuration, day-only calibration buys nothing. One
pairing, one seed, and not a verdict input.

## What the night actually produced

Nothing shipped and the number did not move. What changed is that three plausible directions are now
closed by measurement rather than open by assumption: prior flattening does not rescue pinned entry,
the end cannot be fixed by proposing candidate ends, and day-only calibration is worth nothing on the
live mode. The gap remains where phase 1 put it, in the half of passes whose interval is wrong, and
no lever tried tonight reaches it without turning the end into a clock.
