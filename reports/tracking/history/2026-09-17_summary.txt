4. WAS THE STALL RENDER OR MODEL, PER PARTICIPANT

MODEL, on 74 of 120 passes. RENDER on 7. It is the decoder that stops, and the drawing was
showing that correctly.

The cause is not what it looks like, and it is not that the decoder is slow. It is COVERAGE. The
bounded decoder decodes only walk-mode windows, a median of 4 out of the 10 labelled pass windows,
and a window's position label sits at its CENTRE. On a 5.00 m line those four centres fall at
0.50 m and 4.25 m and two points between. The first 0.50 m and last 0.75 m of every walk are
labelled walk_edge, walking without a position, and are withheld from the decode.

Measured over the 72 straight-line passes: at its last question the decoder says 4.25 m and the
person is at 4.25 m. It is 0.00 m behind. It does not reach its own speed cap on a single one of
the 72 passes, and the cruising speed of 1.250 m/s is slower than the 1.298 m/s the model assumes.
The whole median shortfall of 0.75 m is walking that happens after the last window it is given.

THE DOT IS NOT WRONG WHERE IT STOPS. It is exactly right about the last moment anyone asked.

  window budget predicts the shortfall      r = +0.807
  emission strength predicts the shortfall  r = -0.025

Per participant, worst first. The emission column runs the WRONG WAY for the obvious explanation:

  participant  emissions  walk windows  shortfall  tracked to end
  P3             1.430      4 of 10      1.32 m        15 %
  P6             1.336      4 of 10      1.32 m        20 %
  P4             1.522      4 of 10      1.00 m        20 %
  P2             1.189      4 of 10      0.91 m        35 %
  P1             1.134      5 of 11      0.50 m        60 %
  P5             1.109      5 of 11      0.00 m        80 %

The two the radio sees BEST, P4 and P3, track worst. The two it sees WORST, P5 and P1, track best.
What separates them is the middle column. P1 and P5 get five walk windows per pass and everyone
else gets four, and one more window is one more transition, which is 1.298 m more reach.

So the answer to "why P3" is that P3 walks the line fast enough to span four windows rather than
five. Stalling is a symptom of walking quickly, not of being hard to see.

One note on the operator's impression: P3 as worst is confirmed. P2 as near perfect is not. P2 is
mid-table at 0.91 m and 35 percent; P5 is the one that is actually near perfect. The eye reports
rank badly, which is why the measurement came first.

Median signed offset, red minus green: +0.000 m. The D1 render fix of 17 September holds and no
constant offset has returned.


5. DID THE FIX SHIP, AND WHAT DID THE GUARD SHOW

THE MODEL FIX DID NOT SHIP.

Forced alignment was implemented as specified, as a flag on the bounded decoder, default off, with
the live unbounded decoder untouched. Three bars were set before it ran. It clears two:

  stall fraction falls to near zero        36.7 -> 100.0 percent tracked to end, stall 0.0    PASS
  error stays within the 0.11 m band       0.323 -> 0.815 m, which is 0.49 m worse            FAIL
  beats dead reckoning on >1 pairing       beats it on 9 of 9, 0.815 m against 1.031 m        PASS

And the cost that decides it: LINE IDENTIFICATION COLLAPSES FROM 65.0 TO 29.2 PERCENT, worse on
9 of 9 cross-day pairings.

THE ABLATION IS THE FINDING WORTH KEEPING. Pinning changes two things at once: it widens the
decode to the whole declared pass, and it pins both ends. A variant doing only the first lands in
exactly the same place:

  decode the walk core only, as shipped    line 65.0 %   err 0.323 m   to end  36.7 %
  widen the window, no pin                 line 29.2 %   err 0.815 m   to end 100.0 %
  widen the window and pin both ends       line 29.2 %   err 0.815 m   to end 100.0 %

The terminal pin costs NOTHING on its own. The WIDENING costs everything: 35.8 line points and
0.49 m. So the restriction to the pass core is not an oversight that causes the stall. It is
LOAD-BEARING. The same four windows that cannot reach the end of the line are what keep the line
decision right, and the stall is the price of the accuracy.

A further check, because it is the obvious worry. If stalling meant the tracker was struggling,
stalled passes should be the ones it gets wrong. They are not:
  tracked to the far end   46 passes   line correct 76.1 %
  stopped short            74 passes   line correct 82.4 %

WHAT DID SHIP is a render change, and only a render change. The drawing never claims more arc
length than the decoder committed, and when a run ends it HOLDS at that last decision for the rest
of the declared walk instead of vanishing to the room centre. Measured over the same 120 passes,
time per pass with red drawn at the room centre while the person is still walking went from
0.30 s to 0.00 s. Metrics are untouched: the 5.2 render summary is identical to 5.1's.
