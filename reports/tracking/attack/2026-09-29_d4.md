## What was tested (`pipeline/constrained/pd_arch.py`)

ONE LinkCNN encoder shared by the three receiver links. It is the recipe's own encoder: 5x5
convolutions 16/32/48, BatchNorm, ReLU, max-pool, adaptive pool 3x3, dropout 0.2, 64-d embedding.
It feeds one of two heads:
- **fuse:** a learned fusion head, IDHead(192 -> cells), on the three embeddings concatenated in
  receiver order;
- **attn:** attention over links. A shared scorer plus a learned per-link bias gives a softmax weight
  per link per window, and the weighted embedding feeds IDHead(64 -> cells).

Both carry a per-link auxiliary head, trained jointly: the loss is the fused head's cross entropy
plus the mean of the three auxiliary ones. Inputs are standardised per link and channel on the
training windows. Training uses D2's options, augmentation and relative RSSI. Decoding uses D3's
configuration: the relative motion gate and the display lock. The per-link networks with mean fusion
are the base.

## Reading

- **Learning the fusion beats averaging per-link posteriors** on every rotation, and most on the
  edge lines. The mean of three separately trained link posteriors throws away how the receivers
  agree. A head that sees all three embeddings at once can learn the geometry: which receiver
  resolves which line.
- **One encoder shared by three receivers also triples the data each weight sees,** and it cannot
  specialise to one receiver's placement, which is what a cross-room model needs.
- **Attention over links did badly.** Weighting and summing the embeddings discards which receiver
  each came from. The fusion head keeps them in order, and receiver identity carries the geometry.
