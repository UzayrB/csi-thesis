# Brief: lean into the 4 s window and stack what works (1 October 2026, late)

## Goal

Build the best gait-based identification from one start press, starting from what worked: the 4.0 s window. It scored 37.78 % [23.33, 53.33], paired +5.56 [+1.67, +10.56] against engine v3, with slow +3.33 and fast +6.72. That is the first lever whose paired interval excludes zero.

Stack further levers on top of it and keep each one only if its losses stay small. The target is at least 40 % on natural walks, with the slow and fast walks no worse than engine v3.

**Hard stop: the end of 2 October,** including the engine build and the teaching pack if a new engine is adopted.

Write a PLAN note with the rules, the arms and the stacking order below before anything runs, and commit it.

## Rules that do not change

- One start press and no end press. No press-timed walk time and no press speed as inputs.
- Nothing from tracking's output, no warm-up, only unlabelled same-day adaptation (fix L layout pools), no new data, names not codes.
- Engine v3's bug fixes stay in. Development evaluation, all data seen.
- **One GPU job at a time, under the watchdog,** as you set up after tonight's crash.
- **Closed, do not retry:** the radio end detector, pace-normalised maps (C1), velocity maps (C2), the cross-session contrastive loss (C3), DANN, and Part B's walk ratio, step length, normalised cadence and contour columns.

## The new rules (these replace the PROMISING rule)

Speed measured from the radio counts as a gait parameter. So the margin over speed alone is **reported, not required**, as long as the slow and fast walks hold.

**The step rule** (a lever stays in the stack only if all of these hold against the stack so far):
1. the natural paired gain is at least +2.22 (4 walks);
2. neither fold falls by more than 3.4 points;
3. neither slow nor fast falls by more than 2.5 points;
4. the walks named PA1 rise by no more than 5.

**The final rule** (for adopting the stack as engine v4):
1. the natural paired change against engine v3 has an interval that excludes zero;
2. slow and fast are each at least engine v3's (25.83 and 23.53);
3. the leave-one-session-out tracking walks (Room A and Room B) are not below v3's (31.67 and 27.50) by more than 4.45;
4. all of this holds with all three seeds.

## Part 0: confirm the base window (cheap: feature retrains and CNN inference)

1. **The plateau check.** 3.5 s gave −0.56 and 4.0 s gave +5.56; a jump that big between neighbours can be luck. Score T = 3.75 and 4.25 s, as well as 3.5, 4.0 and 4.5. The 4.0 s window is only trusted if the windows on either side of it (3.75 and 4.25 s) are within 2.5 points of it.
2. **A window that depends on direction.** The direction is known at 3 s. On walks towards the TX the strongest RX2 signal comes in the last second, near RX2; on walks away it comes in the first seconds.
   - Report each window's accuracy separately for towards and away.
   - Then score the rule "towards uses its best window, away uses its best window". It is deployable, because the app decides whether to wait longer after it knows the direction.
3. **A multi-window ensemble.** The equal sum of the evidence from 3.0, 3.5, 4.0 and 4.5 s, each with its own feature components, decided at 4.5 s.
4. **The base.** It is the best of 4.0 s, the direction rule and the multi-window ensemble that passes the step rule against v3 and the plateau check. If none passes, the base is 4.0 s.
   - Score the base on the leave-one-session-out tracking walks at once, so a window that only helps the identification walks shows up now.

## Part 1: cheap levers on the base (seconds each)

Score each one against the base, then stack them in Part 3.

1. **One temperature per component, cross-fitted between the folds.** At 3 s it gained +3.33 and was positive on both folds.
2. **Choose features by how stable they are across days.** Gait studies choose biometric parameters by test–retest reliability.
   - For each of the 630 features, compute on the fold's training sessions only the ratio of between-person variance to the same person's variance across sessions (an ICC).
   - Keep the top k (150, 300 and 450), and retrain the five feature components.
3. **RX2 features that do not depend on its exact placement.**
   - RX2 was moved beside the TX and back for every participant's slot, so each person's identification walks have their own RX2 placement.
   - The forest's most important features, and the LDA's largest pulls towards PA1, are RX2 amplitude-level columns: RSSI spread and modulation depths. These depend on exact placement, while Doppler frequency hardly does.
   - Drop RX2's amplitude-level columns (list them) and keep its frequency-domain columns.
4. **RX1's shadowing and sway.** RX1 never moved and is the strongest single link (31.11 % alone at 3 s). The walker blocks the TX–RX1 line of sight for the whole walk, and the body's sway modulates that blockage at the step rate. Add:
   - the attenuation profile resampled from time to distance using the Wi-Fi speed: its depth, width and asymmetry;
   - RX1's power at the resolved step rate in the gait band.
5. **Session bias correction over the same-layout pool.** At 3 s it lifted B2 by +6.67, exactly where 4.0 s loses. It assumes everyone walks equally often, so flag that beside its number.

## Part 2: the networks on the base maps (one job at a time)

1. **Retrain the CNN components on the base window's maps, all three seeds.** Seed 0 at 4.0 s gave 38.89 % against Part A's 37.78 % with the 3 s weights.
2. **A whole-window CNN.** One input covering the whole base map instead of 34-frame (1.81 s) windows, so the network pools over every gait cycle in the walk. Seed 0, against the same-seed baseline.
3. **Mild pace augmentation, consistent with the physics.** During training only, scale each map's frequency axis by s and its time axis by 1/s, with s drawn uniformly from 0.92 to 1.08.
   - Unlike C1, it keeps speed in the map.
   - It teaches the network to tolerate the few-percent change in a person's pace from one day to the next.
   - Seed 0, against the same-seed baseline.

## Part 3: stack in this fixed order

1. the base;
2. the retrained CNNs (2.1);
3. the temperatures (1.1);
4. the better of the stable-feature selection (1.2) and the placement-robust RX2 features (1.3), then the other;
5. RX1's shadowing and sway (1.4);
6. the better of the whole-window CNN (2.2) and pace augmentation (2.3), then the other;
7. the bias correction (1.5), last and flagged.

Keep each step only if the step rule holds against the stack so far. Then score the final stack:
- all three seeds;
- the leave-one-session-out tracking walks;
- slow and fast;
- S0 and S_wifi on natural, slow and fast;
- per-person accuracy, PA1 and PA2;
- the decision time.

Apply the final rule.

## Part 4: diagnostics for the write-up (information only, cheap)

1. **Are the errors systematic for a person on a given day?** For each person and test day, report the share of their wrong walks that receive the same wrong name. On v3, combining 3 walks gives 38.89 % and 5 walks 36.11 %, barely above one walk, which suggests a person's walks on a given day consistently look like someone else's.
2. **Same day against across days, link by link.** Run the AC2 recipe with the feature components only, on RX1's, RX2's and RX3's columns separately. If the same-day advantage concentrates in RX2, the "recording" confound is largely RX2's per-slot placement.
